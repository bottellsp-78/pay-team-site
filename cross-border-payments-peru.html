03 Peru in detail

What you need to know.

Wallets are the last mile in Peru

Peru’s central bank pushed hard on retail payment interoperability, and the result is a market where mobile wallets carry a very large share of everyday transactions and connect to the wider banking system rather than sitting in silos.

For a business paying suppliers, contractors or sellers, this matters: reach is no longer limited to counterparties with a traditional bank account, and the domestic leg is fast enough that it stops being the delay.

What we actually solve for

The difficulty in Peru is rarely the local rail. It is finding a counterparty that combines proper licensing, competitive FX on PEN, and reach across both banks and wallets — then keeping documentation clean enough that customs and your auditors are satisfied.

That is a placement problem, and it is what we do. We have placed global automotive manufacturers and household-name online businesses into these corridors. Same rails, same desks, same compliance file.

Questions

Straight answers.

Can I pay Peruvian suppliers in soles without a local entity?

Yes. Licensed institutions can convert and settle domestically in PEN while you fund in your own currency, with the FX contract documented in your name.

How fast is settlement to Peru?

Domestic legs clear quickly through Peru’s interoperable network. Within the FX cut-off, same-day settlement is achievable; outside it, next business day.

Can you reach beneficiaries who only use a mobile wallet?

Yes. Peru’s interoperability work means wallet and bank beneficiaries can both be reached, which matters given how much of the market now runs on wallets.

Is Peru on the FATF grey list?

No. Peru is not among the jurisdictions under increased monitoring. Standard trade due diligence applies.
