03 Colombia in detail

What you need to know.

Bre-B is the real change

Banco de la República built Bre-B as an interoperable instant payments system, and full interoperability across participating institutions went live in September, letting users move money using a simple alias rather than account details. Adoption has been fast — the system passed 500 million transactions, and it has been recognised internationally for its design.

For cross-border, the significance is that the last mile stopped being slow. Paying a Colombian supplier or a few thousand Colombian sellers is now a question of how quickly the FX leg clears, not how long the local banking system takes.

The part that still needs doing properly

Colombia regulates who may execute foreign exchange. The conversion has to run through a licensed exchange market intermediary, with the operation classified and reported correctly. Get that wrong and the speed of Bre-B is irrelevant — the funds are still exposed.

We place clients with intermediaries that combine the licence with genuine Bre-B reach, so the regulatory leg and the fast leg are handled by counterparties that actually work together.

Questions

Straight answers.

What is Bre-B?

Bre-B is Colombia’s interoperable instant payment system, built by Banco de la República. It lets payments move between participating banks and wallets in seconds using a registered alias, and it has already processed over 500 million transactions.

Can I pay Colombian suppliers the same day?

Usually yes. Bre-B settles the domestic leg in seconds, so within the FX cut-off same-day settlement is routine. The constraint is the foreign exchange leg, not the local rail.

Do I need a Colombian entity to pay in COP?

No. A licensed exchange market intermediary can convert and settle domestically in COP on your behalf while you fund in your own currency.

Is Colombia a high-risk jurisdiction?

Colombia is not on the FATF list of jurisdictions under increased monitoring. Standard trade due diligence applies.
